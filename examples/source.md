# Source document — Data integrity requirements for laboratory records

## 21 CFR Part 11

Section 11.10(e) requires the "use of secure, computer-generated,
time-stamped audit trails to independently record the date and time of
operator entries and actions that create, modify, or delete electronic
records." Record changes shall not obscure previously recorded information.
Audit trail documentation must be retained as long as the subject records
and be available for agency review and copying.

## EU GMP Annex 11

The current Annex 11 came into force in June 2011. Clause 9 is risk-based
rather than absolute: "Consideration should be given, based on a risk
assessment, to building into the system the creation of a record of all
GMP-relevant changes and deletions."

A draft revision was published on 7 July 2025 and the consultation closed
on 7 October 2025. The draft expands the annex from 5 pages to 19 pages
across 17 chapters, with roughly ten subsections on audit trails alone.
It requires capture of who, what (old and new values), when (including
time zone), and why.

## MHRA GXP Data Integrity Guidance

Revision 1 was published in March 2018. It defines ALCOA+ as attributable,
legible, contemporaneous, original and accurate, plus complete, consistent,
enduring and available. It states that audit trails "should be switched on"
and that "users should not be able to amend or switch off the audit trail."

## ISO/IEC 17025:2017

Clause 7.5 covers technical records. Amendments must be tracked to previous
versions or original observations, retaining both the original and the
amendment, including the date of alteration, what was altered, and the
personnel responsible. Clause 7.11 requires that information management
systems be validated for functionality before use.

## Enforcement

An analysis of 85 FDA warning letters issued between 1 January and
9 December 2025 found that 15% cited data integrity concerns. The
geographic split was India 60%, China 21% and the United States 10%.
87% of the letters recommended engaging a GMP consultant.

In May 2013 Ranbaxy agreed to pay 500 million dollars in total, comprising
a 130 million dollar criminal fine, a 20 million dollar forfeiture and
350 million dollars in civil settlements.
